Foundations & Decision Framework

Purpose: The starting point for every visualization decision — why plots matter, how to classify your variables, and the master framework used throughout this reference.

1. Why Visualization Matters

A dataset can be summarized numerically, modeled statistically, or visualized. These approaches are complementary rather than competing.

  • Summary statistics: mean, median, standard deviation, quantiles, proportions, etc. They compress data into numbers.
  • Statistical models: regression and other models quantify relationships, effects, uncertainty, or prediction.
  • Visualization: exposes distribution shape, unusual observations, clusters, trends, non-linearity, and patterns that summary statistics can hide.

The Datasaurus Dozen Lesson

The Datasaurus Dozen contains datasets with essentially matching summary statistics—including similar means, standard deviations, and correlations—while having radically different shapes. The practical lesson is fundamental:

Never assume that similar summary statistics imply similar data structure. Plot the data.

Visualization is therefore part of exploratory data analysis (EDA), model diagnostics, communication, and decision-making.

2. Variable Types

Continuous / Quantitative Variables

Numeric measurements for which arithmetic and meaningful differences are generally meaningful, such as:

  • Height
  • Temperature
  • Revenue
  • Age
  • Price
  • Area

Categorical Variables

Values representing groups or labels, such as:

  • Country
  • Department
  • Industry
  • Eye color
  • Product category

Variables Whose Treatment Depends on the Question

Some variables can legitimately be treated differently depending on analytical intent.

  • Age: continuous as an exact number; categorical when grouped into age bands.
  • Time: continuous/ordered when studying a time series; categorical when comparing named months or reporting periods as discrete groups.

The same raw field can therefore lead to different visualizations depending on the question.

Line plotOrdered/sequential observationsHow does a measure change across a sequence?Trend, changes, turning points, trajectories

Dot plotCategorical + metricHow do category-level values compare?Position of values; supports log scales and multiple metrics
