B. Why you must plot the data

The Datasaurus lesson from Foundations: matching means, SDs and correlation do not imply matching structure.

Never assume that similar summary statistics imply similar data structure.

C. Core grammar

D. Integrity

Truncated bars manufacture drama

22.5% vs 21.0% is a 1.5 percentage-point gap. A chopped axis can make it look like a landslide.

Dual axis vs small multiples

flowchart TD
M[Two metrics, incompatible units] --> X{Need a single visual relationship?}
X -->|Usually no| P[Two panels. Shared X if time. Honest Y scales.]
X -->|Tempted by dual axis| W[Stop. Changing either scale changes the apparent correlation.]

G. Constitution — wall card

  1. Question first, chart second.
  1. Position before color, size, angle, area.
  1. Bar length is magnitude; ordinary bars start at zero.
  1. No decorative 3D.
  1. Color family must match data semantics.
  1. Do not imply order where none exists.
  1. Do not invent a midpoint.
  1. Heatmaps summarize linear association only.
  1. Facet when a third variable would otherwise muddy the view.
  1. Polar coordinates are for genuine circularity.
  1. Prefer panels over dual axes.
  1. Remove chartjunk, keep context.
  1. Optimize for insight speed.
  1. A beautiful chart that hides the truth is a bad visualization.
