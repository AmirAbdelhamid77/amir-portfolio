Part XII — Quick Reference

12.1 Most Important Rules

  1. Start with the decision, not the chart.
  1. Know your audience before choosing your level of detail.
  1. Define one Big Idea.
  1. Use the minimum sufficient evidence.
  1. Choose metrics based on the question.
  1. Choose visualizations based on the message and audience.
  1. Every visualization should have a job.
  1. Explain the insight; do not make the audience discover it alone.
  1. Always connect Insight → Impact → Action.
  1. Use simple language for non-technical stakeholders.
  1. Do not confuse statistical significance with business significance.
  1. Do not aggregate away important problems.
  1. Less is more when the removed content does not improve the decision.
  1. Use Oral communication for interaction; Written communication for persistence and depth.
  1. Combine formats when persuasion and documentation are both required.

12.2 Mental Models

Framework A — The Complete Mental Model

BUSINESS QUESTION ↓ AUDIENCE / PERSONA ↓ PURPOSE / DECISION ↓ BIG IDEA ↓ SELECT RIGHT FINDINGS ↓ SELECT RIGHT METRICS ↓ SELECT RIGHT VISUALS ↓ BUILD NARRATIVE ↓ CHOOSE FORMAT + CHANNEL ↓ DELIVER ↓ FEEDBACK / QUESTIONS ↓ DECISION ↓ ACTION

Framework B — Data → Decision → Action (Integrated)

BUSINESS PROBLEM ↓ DEFINE THE QUESTION ↓ UNDERSTAND THE AUDIENCE ↓ SELECT THE RIGHT DATA ↓ CHOOSE APPROPRIATE METRICS/STATISTICS ↓ ANALYZE ↓ FIND THE CENTRAL INSIGHT ↓ BUILD THE NARRATIVE ↓ SELECT THE FORMAT (Report or Presentation) ↓ COMMUNICATE ↓ UNDERSTANDING ↓ DECISION ↓ ACTION

Ultimate principle: The best data story is not the one that contains the most analysis. It is the one that communicates the most important, trustworthy insight to the right audience, in the simplest effective way, and helps them make a better decision.

The analyst's mental model: Your job is not merely to show the analysis. Your job is to guide a stakeholder from a business problem to an evidence-backed insight, then to a decision and, when appropriate, an action.

12.3 Which framework, when

Use the selector page instead of stacking every model into one deck.

  • Diagnose → What / So what / Now what + 3-minute story
  • Choose → SCQA or Setup–Stakes–Shift
  • Inspire → Duarte Sparkline + Dykes climax
  • Causal chain → Pixar because of that, then check mix / Simpson / denominator
  • No decision owner → do not story

Framework Selector — Knaflic, Dykes, Duarte, SCQA

12.3 Source Concepts Covered

  • Storytelling with Data — Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic: Three-Minute Story and Big Idea concepts.
  • Effective Data Storytelling — Brent Dykes: narrative structure and audience-oriented communication concepts.
  • ADEPT technique — Kalid Azad: analogies, diagrams, examples, plain language, and technical depth.
  • McCandless Method — David McCandless: headline, anticipate questions, explain insight, and make the insight relevant.
  • Duarte Sparkline — what is ↔ what could be; audience as hero.
  • McKinsey SCQA / Pyramid — Situation, Complication, Question, Answer; lead with the recommendation.
  • Setup–Stakes–Shift–Stake-out — committee vote grammar, including what we accept at no.
  • Pixar story spine — causal chain only when the because-of-that survives an integrity check.
