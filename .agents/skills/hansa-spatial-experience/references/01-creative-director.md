# 01 — Creative Director: invention, divergence, deliberate authorship

## Creative mandate
Create exceptional, legible web interfaces whose arrangement and motion are uniquely driven by content. Never settle for a stock component layout solely because it builds or matches the library. Explicitly distinguish **visual novelty**, **interactive utility**, **learning value**, **spatial craft**, **brand coherence**, and **factual fidelity**.

### Discovery interview (answer from task/repo, not invented user claims)
- Who is looking? What do they already know? What do they need to figure out or decide?
- What must be *noticed immediately*, *explored next*, and *remembered later*?
- Is the information a hierarchy, graph, sequence, comparison, distribution, story, process, uncertainty, or argument?
- What can movement reveal that a static presentation cannot?
- What emotional register should dominate: contemplative, precise, futuristic, tactile, kinetic, archival, playful, architectural, luxurious, or experimental?
- Which local layouts have become stale (especially cards of similar radius with soft gradients)?

## Divergence engine (use at least 4 directions on flagship work)
Draw from non-overlapping families:
1. Architectural sculpture: gallery hall, object stage, corridor, layered chambers, glass volumes.
2. Organic growth: branching, generative filaments, morphogenesis, cell division, crystal accretion.
3. Navigable cosmos: constellations, orbital clusters, gravitational focus, navigation map.
4. Mechanical lab: exploded diagrams, layered instruments, rack of modules, synchronized mechanisms.
5. Editorial theater: kinetic headlines, split-stage reveals, typographic mass/scale, animated narrative.
6. Information terrain: mountains of relationships, strata, topological fields and spatial density.
7. Temporal theater: time ribbons, branching decisions, history scrub, causal replay.
8. Spatial documentary: guided exhibits and annotated camera moves through source-backed evidence.

### Diversity test
A/B/C/D must vary along >= 4 axes: metaphor, layout topology, viewpoint, motion language, interaction model, material/lighting signature, density profile, spatial dimension. Changing colors on the same orbit diagram is **not** divergence.

## Authoring cycle
**Study → sketch → produce four thumbnails (in text, drawing or browser) → choose two → prototype → inspect frames/video → reject weak → polish → interaction QA**. Seek hero composition, clear hierarchy and multi-layer richness before adding sparkles. Use actual screenshots and interactions, not adjectives, as evidence.

## Creative failure modes
- Mistaking blur, gradient, hover tilt and glowing cards for original architecture.
- Overusing the same radial node diagram for unrelated content.
- Placing 3D behind everything as wallpaper, where text readability suffers.
- Copying inspiration literally or reusing another creator's trade dress/assets.
- 'Premium' = dark + purple + glow; 'scientific' = blue cubes; 'organic' = particles everywhere.
- Telling a user the experience is cinematic when no visual staging, camera changes or scene-to-scene rhythm exist.

## Refine rather than settle
Ask: Can the hero silhouette be recognized in grayscale? Does the eye travel deliberately? Is there a memorable interaction linked to actual content? Is the scene materially distinctive? Can the interface carry dense realistic data? Does the mobile version become a considered composition? If any answer is no, redesign rather than polishing tokens.
