# 24 — How to turn inspiration into an original interface

## Process
1. Identify the source object, author, medium, and what was actually observed. A static poster does not prove motion engine or interactions.
2. Decompose into transferable principles: contrast, semantics, visual metaphor, negative space, material, choreography, framing, tension between rigid/organic systems.
3. Identify which source properties are distinctive copyrighted art vs general design concepts. Avoid reproducing identifiable composition, imagery, assets or code.
4. Map concept to Hansa needs. For 'Literal File Tree' style inspiration, translate 'a familiar data structure made organic' into a *source-backed semantic knowledge graph* with non-hierarchical edges; do not copy Finder window or the exact tree form.
5. Generate at least four independent concepts so the result is not a single imitation.
6. Draw an original composition and design vocabulary, choose 3D/2.5D based on semantics, and benchmark art direction + interaction on real content.
7. Cite/credit inspiration in private design notes as appropriate, without implying an official collaboration or licensed clone.

## Verify vs infer
A photo/poster can verify layout, color, apparent material, composition and visible controls. It cannot verify renderer choice, GPU shaders, interactive affordances, full animation timings, keyboard support or performance unless inspected in working implementation. Explicitly mark observations vs hypotheses.

## Transferable lessons from experimental visual work
- Meaningful visual metaphor can be stronger than expensive technology.
- Unexpected contrast (rigid technical frame vs organic internal form) creates identity.
- Negative space increases emphasis without resorting to noisy effects.
- Sophisticated richness comes from spatial staging, causality and materials, not maximum particle count.
