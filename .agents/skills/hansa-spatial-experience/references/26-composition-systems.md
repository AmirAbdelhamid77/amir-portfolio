# 26 — Composition systems: creative richness without UI repetition

A site may have the world's best animation library and still look flat if all content enters a centered max-width grid of rectangular cards. Define visual structure before components.

2. Museum corridor — sequential spatial chapters, accessible jump index.
3. Split precision / spectacle — 3D mechanism juxtaposed with exact data.
4. Layered technical drafting table — overlapping annotated engineering planes.
5. Off-axis theatre — asymmetry and a bold foreground focal object.
6. Expanding tree garden — typed hierarchy grows into negative space.
7. Multi-cluster constellation — relational groups and controlled local focus.
8. Vertical excavations — history or dependency strata peel into depth.
9. Ring laboratory — phase controls around a central working mechanism.
10. Side-by-side comparison chambers — two systems stay metrically aligned.
11. Suspended object gallery — curated projects occupy a spatial stage.
12. Topographic section — valid spatial or conceptual layers under cutaway.
13. Folded editorial poster — typography wraps and reveals information in depth.
14. Kinetic split headline — direct link between title and interactive image.
15. Orbit-to-panel handshake — object turns into anchored readable detail.
16. Progressive scene portal — reveal subsequent stage without losing navigation.
17. Dark theatre with one luminous signal — high focal hierarchy, sparse effects.
18. Bright paper sculpture — tactile depth on light surface, no neon dependency.
19. Mechanical exploded anatomy — stages arranged on z-axis.
20. Interactive diagram cabinet — users open subsets rather than confront dense full diagram.
21. Reflective architectural hall — strong material identity with content index.
22. Schematic fabric tapestry — thread connections form verified lineage.

## A compositional design recipe
- **Hero silhouette:** one object recognizable immediately; don't scatter emphasis evenly.
- **Information density:** determine where dense facts reside (often DOM panel), where exploratory connections live (scene), and where actions live (controls).
- **Scale hierarchy:** dominant/secondary/tertiary objects with intentional dramatic ratio; avoid 20 equally important objects.
- **Rhythm:** alternate intimate detail and expansive overview, fast feedback and calm resting states.
- **Spatial cue stack:** overlap + projection + light + parallax + material. Use enough to communicate scene, but not so much that content is lost.
- **Interaction payoff:** a first click reveals a concept/transition deeper than mere color change.
- **Mobile recut:** select a new composition type from this list instead of shrinking the desktop composition.

## Diversity matrix
Across consecutive major site sections avoid repeating simultaneously:
- topology (grid/orbit/ribbon/corridor/cluster),
- hero placement (center/right/off-axis/full scene),
- background treatment (dark black/white paper/textured/matte),
- material (glass/metal/paper/resin/soft organic),
- camera (orthographic/perspective/2.5D/no camera),
- interaction (scrub/click-to-reveal/direct manipulation/comparison/stepper),
- timing (quiet/kinetic/staged/scroll-controlled).

## Critique on realism
Mock screen design on realistic long labels, 80 node graph fixture, 6 lines of code, 3 cited paragraphs, 320px viewport, reduced motion. Unrealistically sparse placeholders disguise design weakness. Examples with invented synthetic data are acceptable only in separate clearly labeled prototype fixtures.
