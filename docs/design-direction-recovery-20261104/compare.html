01Timeline

Composition and reading rhythm lost. The prototype is a date you can move through: fanned edition cards, ghost numerals, cables to a ruler, a selected card, a peek and steppers. The shipped strip is a row of equal ticks with three coloured bars — clearer about review status, but no longer a place.

Removed

Fan geometry and scale falloff, ghost date numerals, four-line cables, five-tick ruler, drag with inertia, horizontal wheel, per-day titles, latest pin, prev/next steppers, status footer.

Keep

One tab stop for the strip, arrow/Home/End/Enter grammar, honest quiet days, review labels, permalink and archive. These are genuine M6 improvements.

02Knowledge scene

The neighbourhood is gone. The prototype opens one idea into a labelled local graph with search, branches, preview and a text fallback. The shipped scene opens the edition’s reasoning — which is good — then hands the “semantic layer” to a 0-published empty state and four links.

Removed

Concept search over labels, aliases and definitions; subject entrances; Requires/Unlocks/Contrasts/Related grouping; hover/focus branch preview; Explore connections vs Open content; expand; same-subject membership; complete table fallback; coverage meta.

Keep and build on

The edition’s situation → reasoning → caveat → source, the decision experiment, the instrument, reveal/evidence toggles, and the fail-closed audience boundary under the UI.

03Dealer

The deal became a scroll. The prototype deals into a full-screen table with an edition rail, the whole hand, and a readable panel for the selected card. The shipped dealer is an inline section that reveals one card at a time and asks you to “Deal 2 more” before you can compare or read them side by side.

Removed

Modal focus containment, the full hand as a scannable set, simultaneous reader, edition day rail, card capability row, per-card save, locate-in-knowledge from the card.

Keep

The cinematic deal moment (it is good), honest empty hands, real card content, caveat and source, and the dealer’s refusal to invent a card.

04Closing

The ledger and the idea left the room. The prototype closes with one sentence from the reading, a single action, the pocket, and a coverage ledger that keeps the denominators honest. The shipped closing is a destination grid plus the archive.

05Mobile

The graph survives on mobile in the prototype. Below 720 px the field becomes a labelled list with the same direction and actions, so the neighbourhood never disappears. The shipped mobile knowledge scene has no concept layer to collapse.

06Atlas

The real atlas already exists — outside the product. With the review preview on, the shipped explorer draws 42 concepts and 49 candidate connections with filters, a circle map, a connection list and an inspector. The public build replaces all of it with a bibliography. The prototype’s neighbourhood is the missing bridge between the edition and that desk.

Gap

Nothing connects an edition to its concept, and the concept to its neighbourhood and the desk. The Home says “0” while the review build says 42/49.

Keep

The explorer’s evidence lens, direction handling, inspector, keyboard map controls, and the no-JS linear atlas. These are worth carrying into the restored experience.

07R1 - the neighbourhood, restored

The missing core is back, behind the review flag. R1 restores the concept, its four labelled groups, the preview strip and the reading panel over the real 42-concept / 49-relation candidate Atlas. It is a dedicated gated route, so the Home is still exactly M6 until R2.

Changed from the prototype

Fair branch budget (one per occupied group before a second), spoken direction sentences, same-kind membership because the corpus has one subject, a placement that uses the whole field, and 10.5-13 px labels instead of 9 px.

Kept

Serif display, paper and ink, four domain accents, the four labelled groups with curved connectors, the preview that carries the claim, explore vs open, expand, membership and the full table fallback.

Measured. 271 tests pass (14 new model guards, including real card boxes for every concept). Axe: 0 violations on the field and on the expanded field. Route JS 27.5 KB raw / 8.4 KB gzip, CSS 4.6 KB gzip, CLS 0. With the flag off, the exported route is a 404 page and the export contains no candidate concept id, title, claim or edge.

08R2 - the edition's neighbourhood

The missing bridge now belongs to the edition. R2 puts the R1 field inside the Home's knowledge scene, under the edition it explains: a breadcrumb, the concept, its labelled branches, search, the panel, and two honest exits (the edition's lesson and practice) plus the Atlas desk. It sits behind a second build flag, so the public Home is still M6 until the owner accepts this and the default changes on purpose.

Two control fixes found while embedding

The branch tag no longer repeats itself (“Related · Related in a decision”), and “Place all N” / “Show fewer” now appear only when something is actually hidden, so an edition with four relations does not offer to expand.

Continuity

Same serif display, paper, ink, four domain accents, labelled groups, curved connectors, preview, fallback and full table. The embed adds only chrome: breadcrumb, edition exits, desk hand-off and a lazier loading state.

Measured. 282 tests pass. With the flag off the exported Home is the same 779 DOM nodes and 42 resources, with no embed, no loading shell and 0 neighbourhood requests; the only delta is +5,680 B of lazy-import stub. With the flag on the slice adds one 21.5 KB JS chunk and 26.1 KB CSS, both fetched only after hydration; LCP 604-960 ms, CLS 0, axe 0 violations on the field and the expanded field, console clean. The R1 route keeps its structure; only the two shared control fixes apply there too.

09R3 - the desktop timeline fan

The timeline is a date you can enter again. R3 restores the prototype's fan on desktop: fifteen cards, the selected edition in the centre, ghost year, playhead, cables, ruler, latest pin and a per-day peek. Moving the fan inspects a date; opening the edition is still a separate action. Below 1024 px, and in the public build, the accepted M6 strip stays exactly as it shipped.

Controls that had to be added or fixed

The prototype's prev/next steppers are back, with disabled ends. The status line now has real · separators, drag and wheel move focus to the settled card, and the fan's CSS lives in its own chunk so the public stylesheet keeps its M6 bytes.

What is deliberately not restored

The strip stays for phones, keyboard-first compact use and the public build. The fan never invents an edition on a quiet day: quiet days are dashed, non-interactive cards, and every gesture settles on the nearest edition.

Measured. 290 tests pass. With the flag off the exported Home is still 779 DOM nodes, 42 resources and 242,150 B of CSS, with no fan markup, no fan link and 0 fan requests; the only delta is +6,783 B of dynamic-import stub. With the flag on the fan is 10,756 B JS + 7,698 B CSS, SSR-rendered in the static HTML, LCP 756 ms, CLS 0, axe 0 violations, console clean. Click, keyboard, drag, wheel and both steppers each change the selection, and 0 overlaps or overflow were found at 1280, 1024, 390 and 320. Accepted by the owner on 2026-11-05; all three flags stay off for the exported site.

10R4 - the dealer as a reading room

The dealer is a hand you read, not a sequence of reveals. R4 restores the prototype's dealer behind its own build-time flag: a modal with the fifteen-day rail, every card of the edition visible at once with its capability tags, and a reader beside it. Selecting a card inspects it; opening the edition is still the timeline's action, and the flag-off public build keeps the accepted inline card table byte for byte.

Controls that had to be added or fixed

The dialog is a portal into document.body — rendered inside the stage tree, the inert background would have contained the dialog itself. Focus returns to the trigger after the background stops being inert, because focusing an inert element is a silent no-op. Announcements are scoped to the dialog, the rail centres with justify-content: safe center, and opening a saved card announces the saved card rather than the deal before it.

What is deliberately not restored

The accepted inline card table stays the public default; the site-wide pocket and the learning thread are deferred to a later slice, so R4's save is an in-dealer bookmark list. The prototype's resource-preview button is not reproduced: the evidence card links directly to the Microsoft Learn source. With the flag off there is no dc- markup, no ?dealer= parsing and no dealer request.

Measured. 302 tests pass, including 12 new dealer guards. With the flag off the exported Home is still 779 DOM nodes, 42 resources and 242,150 B of CSS, with 0 dc- markup and 0 dealer requests; the accepted inline table still deals 1/3 → 3/3 and closes. The only delta is +12,321 B of dynamic-import stub over the M6 baseline (+5,538 B over R3). With the flag on, opening the dealer fetches 11,219 B JS + 12,400 B CSS (a lazy chunk the initial page never requests); LCP stays 844 ms, CLS 0, axe 0 violations, console clean. Focus trap wraps both ways, Escape restores the trigger, 11/12 body children are inert while open, and 0 horizontal overflow was found at 1280, 1024, 390 and 320. Owner acceptance pending; R5 does not start until this slice is accepted.

11R5 - the closing scene

The stage has to end with an argument, not a signpost. R5 restores the prototype's close behind its own build-time flag: one idea taken from the edition the reader selected, one action that reopens it, the cards kept in this browser, and a coverage ledger that says what was read and what was not. The destinations stay, but they move below the argument. With the flag off the accepted M6 close is still the only closing scene.

Corrections found while restoring

The prototype's two-line title concatenated into one ideaand one honest action for assistive technology; the review close carries an explicit space between the lines (the accepted timeline heading has the same defect and is recorded as an R6 candidate rather than changed silently here). The ledger uses <dl> rows so a number is associated with its label, not merely adjacent. The site preflight strips list markers, so the claims set their own quiet dash. The kept list is a sibling of the dealer dialog, so the dealer now dispatches a same-tab hansa:dealer-saved event after every persist.

What is deliberately not restored

The prototype's site-wide pocket and About modal stay deferred: R5 lists the dealer's own saved cards and sends the coverage question to the Atlas desk. The quote is the edition's reviewed takeaway rather than the prototype's evidence quote, because that is the field the review actually approved. With the flag off the closing scene renders the accepted M6 markup and the review close's rules are dead bytes in the one stage stylesheet - measured, not hidden.

Measured. 312 tests pass, including 10 new closing guards. With the flag off the Home renders 0 .stage-close-* nodes and the accepted close is intact; the public build costs 780 DOM nodes (+1 script tag), 43 resources (+1), 827,336 B of script (+10,956 B) and 248,019 B of CSS (+5,869 B, of which 5,599 B is the review close's 50 rules), LCP 756 ms, CLS 0. With the flag on the scene renders as content: 1,339 DOM nodes, 47 resources, LCP 1,264-1,332 ms, axe 0 violations, console clean, and 0 horizontal overflow at 1280, 1024, 390 and 320. Reopen, the coverage disclosure, the kept-list sync and the saved-card open were all exercised; Escape returns focus to the action. Owner acceptance pending; R6 does not start until this slice is accepted.

12R6 - the polish layer

The stage had sixteen type sizes, and a third of its text was shouting. R6 began as a measurement, not a design. The flag-off export set text at 64, 41, 38.4, 30.7, 18, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12.5, 12, 11.5, 11, 10, 9.5 and 9 px; 46 of its 139 text elements (33.1%) were mono and either uppercase or widely tracked, including content values such as TWO TEAMS; three targets sat under 44 px; and the first and last scenes read as frames around 29-32% empty space. The topbar question was answered by the same probe: the fixed header is 57 px and the first scene starts at 152 px, so there was no overlap to fix. Everything below is one review layer behind a sixth flag, HANSA_POLISH_REVIEW=1.

Corrections found while measuring

The first capture set the clock tick to the 11 px label step, and THU 05 NOV wrapped inside its 76 px cell - the tick keeps a 10 px step of its own, and the build still has no 9 px or 9.5 px text. The stage carries the flag attribute itself, so a descendant selector would have matched nothing and silently done nothing; a guard now fails on that shape. One attempted instrument rule was written as a descendant of .hansa-stage, matched nothing, and left a 12 px switch label in the capture - the screenshot caught it, not the tests. Two heading defects recorded during R5 ship in the public build rather than behind the visual flag: the h1's missing space (Choose a moment.Follow the thread.) and the reading h2 that repeated the peek title word for word.

What R6 deliberately does not do

No new family, colour, radius, card, icon or illustration, and no motion: the reduced-motion inventory is 0 animations and 0 transitions before and after. No content, data, publication state or link changes. The review close inherits the 13 px floor when both flags are on but is not redesigned here. With the flag off every R6 rule is dead bytes in the existing stage stylesheet - the same trade R5 made, measured rather than assumed away.

Measured. 320 tests pass, including 8 new polish guards. With the flag off the exported Home renders no data-stage-polish attribute and the accepted M6 type: 16 distinct sizes, 46 of 139 mono labels (33.1%), 3 targets under 44 px, LCP 776 / 792 / 1,152 ms across three runs, CLS 0. With the flag on: 9 sizes (64 / 41 / 32 / 19 / 16 / 14 / 13 / 11 / 10), 26 of 140 mono labels (18.6%), no text under 13 px except labels and meta, 0 targets under 44 px, section fill 73 / 89 / 72 (was 71 / 89 / 68), axe 0 violations and 0 console errors at 1280 dark/light, 1024, 390 and 320. The layer costs +5,041 B of CSS (30 rules, all scoped) and +1 DOM node (the sr-only reading prefix); the polish build itself adds no request, no chunk and no runtime work. A manual NVDA/VoiceOver pass is NOT RUN - the Chromium accessibility tree is recorded, but it is not an AT test. Owner acceptance pending; the flag stays off in the shipped export.
